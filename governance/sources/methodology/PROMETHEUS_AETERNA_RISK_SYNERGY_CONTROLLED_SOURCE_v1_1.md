# 1. PURPOSE

AETERNA RISK SYNERGY defines the methodological discipline through which PROMETHEUS may analyse investment and project risk using public actuarial, insurance, reinsurance, catastrophe-modelling, credit-risk, project-finance, capital-modelling and enterprise-risk-management principles.

Its purpose is not to prove that regenerative investments are safe.

Its purpose is to determine, for each material loss mechanism:

1. what can go wrong;
2. why it can go wrong;
3. what is exposed;
4. how vulnerable the exposure is;
5. how often the loss may occur, where estimable;
6. how severe the loss may be;
7. what dependencies or common shocks can amplify the loss;
8. what can be prevented;
9. what can be mitigated;
10. what can be diversified;
11. what can be absorbed;
12. what can be transferred;
13. the cost and fragility of that transfer;
14. what remains retained;
15. what remains unmodelled;
16. who ultimately bears the loss;
17. how much capital may be permanently impaired;
18. what evidence must exist before more capital is released.

The target is:

> **maximum loss visibility + maximum economically rational prevention + explicit risk allocation + minimum permanent capital impairment + explicit residual uncertainty.**

# 4. AETERNA RISK ONTOLOGY

Every material risk must be represented through the following chain:

**Hazard  
→ Trigger  
→ Exposure  
→ Vulnerability  
→ Direct Loss  
→ Secondary Loss  
→ Frequency / Probability  
→ Severity  
→ Recovery Time  
→ Correlation / Dependence  
→ Preventive Control  
→ Mitigation  
→ Resilience  
→ Diversification  
→ Absorption  
→ Transfer  
→ Retention  
→ Residual Risk  
→ Unmodelled Risk  
→ Ultimate Risk Bearer.**

## 4.1 Definitions

**Hazard** — a condition or process capable of causing loss.  
**Trigger** — the event or threshold that activates the loss mechanism.  
**Exposure** — the asset, cash flow, right, liability, population, ecosystem or operation at risk.  
**Vulnerability** — the conditional susceptibility of the exposure to the hazard.  
**Direct Loss** — first-order financial or physical damage.  
**Secondary Loss** — consequential loss, interruption, financing cost, lost revenue, reputational or systemic consequence.  
**Frequency/Probability** — expected occurrence rate or scenario probability, only where evidence permits.  
**Severity** — conditional loss distribution given occurrence.  
**Recovery Time** — time required to restore a defined operating or financial state.  
**Dependence** — correlation, tail dependence, common factor, network or common-mode relationship with other risks.  
**Prevention** — intervention that reduces or eliminates the hazard/exposure mechanism before loss.  
**Mitigation** — intervention that reduces loss conditional on the event.  
**Resilience** — capacity to absorb, adapt and recover without unacceptable impairment.  
**Diversification** — reduction of aggregate risk through imperfect dependence, never assumed.  
**Absorption** — reserves, liquidity, equity, subordinated capital or other internal capacity.  
**Transfer** — insurance, reinsurance, guarantee, hedge, contractual indemnity or other external allocation.  
**Retention** — risk deliberately or involuntarily kept.  
**Residual Risk** — identified risk remaining after controls and transfer.  
**Unmodelled Risk** — unknown, structurally uncertain or insufficiently evidenced downside.  
**Ultimate Risk Bearer** — the stakeholder that remains economically exposed after all contractual and capital layers are traced.

# 8. PUBLIC REINSURANCE / INSURANCE REFERENCE FRAME

AETERNA uses public disclosures from leading insurers and reinsurers as methodological anchors. It does **not** claim access to proprietary models, pricing engines, exposure databases, underwriting manuals or confidential loss data.

The public-source synthesis supports the following methodological principles:

## 8.1 Munich Re

Use public Annual Reports, SFCRs, loss triangles and risk disclosures as reference material for enterprise-wide risk management, diversification, capital adequacy, risk appetite, underwriting discipline and reinsurance risk.

Permitted inference: Tier-1 reinsurers model multiple material risk families and manage capital against aggregate risk.

Forbidden inference: any unpublished Munich Re pricing formula, internal distribution, proprietary catastrophe model or confidential underwriting rule.

## 8.2 Hannover Re

Public disclosures describe an internal capital model used to monitor enterprise risk and explicit risk-tolerance objectives, including probability-based limits on adverse outcomes.

Permitted inference: risk appetite should be expressed through measurable loss/capital tolerances and monitored through an internal model.

Forbidden inference: reproduce Hannover Re's proprietary model or calibration.

## 8.3 SCOR

Public disclosures describe comprehensive risk management, concentration monitoring, risk appetite, tolerance limits, dependency/accumulation management and capital/solvency discipline.

Permitted inference: concentration, accumulation and interdependence must be explicit rather than hidden in an average score.

## 8.4 Swiss Re

Public Financial Condition Reports disclose use of an internal model that reflects accumulation and diversification between individual risks and reports tail-risk measures.

Permitted inference: standalone risk capital and aggregate risk capital must be separated, and diversification benefit must be measured rather than assumed.

## 8.5 Lloyd's

Public capital guidance and Annual Reports require capital sufficient for severe tail outcomes, including a 99.5% one-year solvency confidence standard and syndicate-level "to ultimate" views; Lloyd's also applies additional capital requirements for its own financial-strength objectives.

Permitted inference: PROMETHEUS should analyse both finite-horizon solvency and ultimate-runoff / long-horizon risk, with scenario-specific buffers where justified.

Forbidden inference: use Lloyd's 1-in-200 or any Lloyd's uplift as a universal PROMETHEUS safety threshold.

## 8.6 Zurich

Public risk reviews describe risk-capital allocation by risk type and use of an internal model for Swiss solvency purposes.

Permitted inference: capital should be mapped by material risk type rather than hidden inside a single composite score.

## 8.7 Everest

Public disclosures describe combining insured/cedant data, internal claims, external data, vendor catastrophe models, actuarial/statistical analysis and in-house views of risk.

Permitted inference: a robust view of risk may use multiple models and data sources, but must retain provenance and disclose model disagreement.

## 8.8 Berkshire Hathaway / Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance

Public reports repeatedly emphasize underwriting discipline, willingness to reject inadequately priced risk, very large-loss awareness, capital strength and long-term ability to pay claims.

Permitted inference: capacity and risk selection are first-class underwriting variables; not every technically priceable risk should be accepted.

Forbidden inference: impute a proprietary Berkshire pricing algorithm from qualitative public statements.

# 10. QUANTITATIVE RISK ENGINE

All critical calculations must be reproducible outside an LLM using deterministic, versioned code.

## 10.1 Aggregate Loss

Where frequency-severity modelling is justified:

\[
S = \sum_{i=1}^{N} X_i
\]

where `N` is event count and `X_i` is event severity.

No independence assumption is allowed unless explicitly justified.

For scenario-based modelling:

\[
EL = \sum_s p_s L_s
\]

where scenario probabilities are defensible. If probabilities are not defensible, report scenario losses without fabricated probabilities.

## 10.2 Expected Loss

\[
EL = E[L]
\]

For credit-style exposures, where valid:

\[
ECL \approx PD \times LGD \times EAD
\]

with explicit time horizon, conditionality and dependency assumptions.

## 10.3 Value at Risk

\[
VaR_\alpha(L)=\inf\{l : P(L \le l)\ge \alpha\}
\]

VaR must never be reported without horizon, confidence level, input distribution and calibration status.

## 10.4 Expected Shortfall

\[
ES_\alpha(L)=E[L \mid L \ge VaR_\alpha(L)]
\]

or its mathematically appropriate equivalent for non-continuous distributions.

AETERNA prefers Expected Shortfall for tail interpretation where the model supports it, while retaining VaR where needed for regulatory or comparative contexts.

## 10.5 Unexpected Loss

AETERNA may define diagnostic unexpected loss as the tail or dispersion amount above expected loss, but the precise definition must be stated for each model.

## 10.6 Cash-Flow-at-Risk

Define the distribution of net cash flow over the specified horizon and report the downside quantile relative to planned or minimum viable cash flow.

## 10.7 Probability of Permanent Capital Impairment — PPCI

PPCI is a PROMETHEUS diagnostic, not an external rating metric.

Before use, define:

- impairment threshold;
- capital base;
- recovery condition;
- recovery horizon;
- treatment of dilution, recapitalisation and insolvency;
- whether nominal or real value is used.

Example conceptual definition:

\[
PPCI(h,\theta)=P(\text{capital impairment at horizon }h \ge \theta \text{ and not recovered under the defined recovery rule})
\]

PPCI must remain `NR` unless the required loss and recovery distributions are supportable.

# 13. DEPENDENCE & AGGREGATION

AETERNA prohibits naïve summation of diversification benefits.

Assess as appropriate:

- Pearson correlation;
- rank correlation;
- common-factor dependence;
- scenario dependence;
- tail dependence;
- copulas;
- network contagion;
- geographic accumulation;
- counterparty concentration;
- supply-chain common points;
- climate common shocks;
- funding-liquidity feedback;
- ecological/financial co-movement.

Diagnostic diversification benefit:

\[
DB = \sum_i RC_i - RC_{agg}
\]

where `RC_i` are standalone risk-capital measures and `RC_agg` is aggregate risk capital under the validated dependence structure.

If dependence is unstable or poorly evidenced, report a range and include a conservative stress case.

# 14. NON-STATIONARITY

Historical observations must not be treated as identically distributed with future observations where structural change is material.

Test for:

- climate trend;
- land-use change;
- regulation;
- inflation;
- technology;
- market regime;
- population/demography;
- input scarcity;
- supplier structure;
- pathogen/pest evolution;
- cyber threat evolution.

Separate:

**Aleatory uncertainty** — intrinsic randomness.  
**Epistemic uncertainty** — incomplete knowledge/model uncertainty.

Do not convert epistemic uncertainty into false probability precision.

# 15. BAYESIAN UPDATING

When evidence is sparse but structured prior information exists:

\[
Posterior(\theta|D) \propto Likelihood(D|\theta)\times Prior(\theta)
\]

Requirements:

- declare prior source;
- test alternative priors;
- show posterior sensitivity;
- distinguish expert prior from empirical prior;
- prevent circular use of the same evidence in prior and likelihood.

Strong conclusions that depend materially on an arbitrary prior are classified as fragile.

# 26. CAPITAL OPTIONALITY

Adopt:

> **Capital not yet irreversibly deployed is downside protection.**

Where uncertainty is material:

1. fund the cheapest rigorous experiment capable of falsifying the key thesis;
2. retain later capital as optionality;
3. release capital only after objective evidence gates;
4. stop, redesign or resize when gates fail;
5. do not use sunk cost as a reason to continue.

This is the **Evidence-Gated Capital Architecture**.

# 46. FINAL CONTROLLED INVARIANT

> **PROMETHEUS shall not ask whether an investment is "safe" in the abstract.
